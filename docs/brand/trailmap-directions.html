01

The idea worth stealing

The Devil’s Thumb map isn’t charming because it’s hand-drawn. It’s charming because every mark on it means something. Hatch ladders are climbs. Chevrons are descents. Arrows are one-way. Stars are benches you can actually sit on. And the Trail Key at the bottom left teaches you the whole language in nine lines.

XC Ski Labs scores 229 races across 14 criteria and currently renders all of it as numbers in boxes. The map hands us a ready-made data language for exactly that job — one every skier already reads without being taught. That’s the part to take. The creased paper is what makes people stop; the legend is what makes the site better than it was.

The rule that keeps it from becoming a costume: a symbol appears only where it carries real data from race-data/*.json. No decorative arrows, no chevrons that aren’t a descent, no stars that aren’t a feed station.

Tier stays typographic (T1–T4). Difficulty glyphs describe the course; tier describes the race. Collapsing them would say a hard race is a good one, which isn’t what the scores mean.

03

What each one costs

The honest version, including the parts that argue against my own recommendation.

A — CartographicB — PasticheC — Paper & glyphs
Feels like the map Yes, structurally. The legend is the map; the geometry is yours. Yes, literally. It looks photographed. Partly. Reads as a palette change more than a redesign.
Reads at 15px Clean Serif and mono do the reading; the hand only labels. Strained A hand face across 229 table rows is genuinely tiring. Clean
Accessibility Shape-encoded difficulty passes without color. Contrast holds at every size. Displacement filters blur stroke edges; the wobble can't be tested for contrast reliably. Unchanged from today — already passing.
Weight on page One new webfont, used sparingly. Glyphs are a shared <defs> block, ~1 KB. Same font at every weight, plus grain and two SVG filters per view. Zero new bytes.
Generator work Token rewrite + a shared trailkey.py the generators import. Real, bounded. Same, plus texture layers and per-element rotations to tune by hand across 15 templates. Token rewrite only. A day.
Ages how Like a good map. The legend keeps earning its place as data grows. Like a themed restaurant. Charming in March, costume by November. Fine, quietly. Nobody will mention it.

My call: A. B is the one that makes you smile in this review and the one you'd get tired of on race page 140. C won't answer the thing you actually said — that you don't like the UI. A takes the map's idea rather than its surface, and it makes the site more useful, not just more charming.

If you want more of B's warmth inside A, the dial is the paper grain and the fold crease — both are single tokens I can turn up without touching the type.

04

How the port runs

Every surface, in the order that keeps the site shippable at each step. Nothing here needs a feature flag — each stage is a green deploy on its own.

  1. Tokenstokens/tokens.css rewritten to the paper palette. Every generator already imports it, so the whole site changes ground on commit one.
  2. Shared trail keyNew wordpress/trailkey.py: the glyph <defs> block, the climb-ladder builder, the legend component, and the difficulty mapper. One source, fifteen consumers.
  3. Race pagesgenerate_race_pages.py — 229 pages, the hardest surface, and the one that proves the criteria ladders. Elevation plates read real vitals.
  4. Homepage and databasegenerate_homepage.py and web/nordic-lab-search.html, plus the copy of the search files into output/search/ that the deploy reads from.
  5. Conversion surfacesCoaching, consulting, apply, intake, questionnaire, training plans, success and confirmation pages — the eleven WordPress generators.
  6. Guide, legal, prep kitThe remaining static surfaces, then generate_feeds.py and OG images.
  7. GuardsToken-only hex rule extended, a legend-vocabulary test that fails if a glyph renders without a backing field, and before/after screenshots on the seven surfaces you review.

The 7,987-test suite stays green throughout; the brand guidelines doc gets rewritten in the same commit as the tokens so the spec never lags the code.
