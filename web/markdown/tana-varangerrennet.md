## Course

Point-to-point across the Finnmark plateau from the inland settlement of Seida to the coastal town of Vadsø on the Varanger fjord. Predominantly open tundra terrain with rolling hills. The course crosses one of Europe's last true wilderness areas, passing through Sámi reindeer-herding territory with vast Arctic horizons in every direction.

Format: point-to-point  
Surface: Machine-groomed classic tracks across Arctic tundra and rolling terrain

## Climate

Arctic tundra winter

April in Finnmark means returning daylight but still deeply cold conditions. The open tundra offers no wind shelter. Temperatures typically -15°C to -5°C. Snow is highly reliable this far north and this late in the season. Wind chill is the primary concern on exposed plateau sections.

Typical race-day temperature: -15 to -5°C

Challenges:
- Extreme wind exposure on open Finnmark tundra
- Wind chill can push effective temperature far below ambient
- Arctic cold demands careful layering and skin protection
- Remote course with limited bail-out options
- 80km distance compounds exposure risk

## History

Founded in 1998, Tana–Varangerrennet is an 80km classic marathon crossing the Finnmark plateau in Norway's far northeast. The race connects the inland Tana valley to the Varanger coast, traversing one of Europe's most remote and dramatic landscapes. Part of the Ski Classics Challengers series, it draws adventurous long-distance skiers to the Arctic tundra.

### Notable Facts

- One of the northernmost long-distance ski races in the world (70°N)
- Crosses traditional Sámi reindeer-herding territory
- Finnmark plateau is larger than Denmark but nearly uninhabited
- Vadsø was historically a center for Finnish immigration (Kven people)
- Part of the Ski Classics Challengers series
- April race date takes advantage of Arctic spring snow and daylight
