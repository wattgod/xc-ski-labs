## Course

Loop course through the high Pyrenean terrain around Baqueira Beret, Spain's most prestigious ski resort. The course traverses Alpine meadows and mountain forest at 1,500-1,800m altitude with significant climbing. Classic technique only — the terrain rewards strong diagonal stride on the climbs.

Format: loop  
Surface: Machine-groomed classic tracks through high Pyrenean terrain

## Climate

High Pyrenean winter

Baqueira Beret sits at 1,500-1,800m in the central Pyrenees, catching Atlantic moisture that delivers heavy snowfall. The Val d'Aran is unique in the Pyrenees — its north-facing Atlantic drainage pattern creates a microclimate more similar to the French side. February temperatures range from -10°C to 2°C.

Typical race-day temperature: -10 to 2°C

Challenges:
- High altitude at 1,500-1,800m affects unacclimatized skiers
- Pyrenean weather can shift rapidly between sun and storm
- Atlantic-influenced snow can be heavier and wetter than Alpine powder
- Challenging terrain with meaningful climbing at altitude

## History

Founded in 1981, the Marxa Beret is Spain's premier cross-country ski race, held in the Val d'Aran — a unique Occitan-speaking valley in the central Pyrenees. The race takes place around Baqueira Beret, Spain's most prestigious ski resort and a favorite of the Spanish royal family. Its inclusion in the Ski Classics Challengers series has elevated its international profile.

### Notable Facts

- Spain's biggest and most prestigious cross-country ski race
- Val d'Aran is the only valley in Spain that drains toward the Atlantic (via the Garonne)
- Aranese, a dialect of Occitan, is co-official with Catalan and Spanish in the valley
- Baqueira Beret is favored by the Spanish royal family for winter holidays
- Part of the Ski Classics Challengers series
- The Pyrenees at this longitude receive heavy Atlantic snowfall — excellent snow record
