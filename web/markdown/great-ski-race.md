## Course

Point-to-point from Tahoe City to Truckee, crossing the North Lake Tahoe ridge. The course traverses backcountry terrain with significant climbing in the first half and a long descent into Truckee. Stunning Sierra Nevada views when conditions permit.

Format: point-to-point  
Surface: Groomed backcountry trails connecting Tahoe City to Truckee  
Technical rating: 3/5  
Grooming: Good grooming for race day, though backcountry sections can be variable

### Features

- Point-to-point crossing of the Sierra Nevada ridge
- West Coast's largest cross-country ski race
- Fundraiser for Nordic search and rescue (Tahoe Nordic SAR)
- Stunning Lake Tahoe and Sierra Nevada views
- Both classic and freestyle racers on course together

## Climate

Sierra Nevada mountain winter

March in the Sierra brings highly variable conditions. The range can receive massive snowfall or enjoy spring-like warmth. Temperatures typically range from -5°C to 8°C. The course crosses ridgeline terrain exposed to Pacific storms. Sierra cement — heavy, wet snow — is common in spring.

Typical race-day temperature: -5 to 8°C

Challenges:
- Sierra Nevada weather is notoriously unpredictable
- Heavy wet snow ('Sierra cement') affects glide and wax
- Ridgeline exposure to Pacific storm winds
- Race has been cancelled or shortened due to extreme weather
- Altitude around 6,200-7,200 feet affects performance

## History

The Great Ski Race has been a North Lake Tahoe tradition since 1977, making it one of the oldest XC ski races in the western United States. Originally conceived as a fundraiser for the Tahoe Nordic Search and Rescue team, it has grown into the West Coast's largest cross-country ski event. The race traverses the ridge between Tahoe City and Truckee, retracing a historic route used by 19th-century mail carriers.

### Notable Facts

- West Coast's largest cross-country ski race since 1977
- Fundraiser for Tahoe Nordic Search and Rescue
- Route follows historic mail carrier trails between Tahoe City and Truckee
- Has been cancelled or modified in low-snow years
- North Lake Tahoe is a major winter sports destination
- Nearly 50 years of continuous operation
