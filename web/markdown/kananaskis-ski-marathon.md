## Course

Loops and out-and-back sections through Kananaskis Country's groomed trail network in the Rocky Mountain foothills. Rolling terrain through lodgepole pine and spruce forest with views of the Rockies. The course follows the provincial park trail system with moderate elevation changes.

Format: loop  
Surface: Machine-groomed trails through subalpine forest  
Technical rating: 3/5  
Grooming: Provincial park grooming, well-maintained but basic compared to World Cup standards

### Features

- Trails wind through old-growth spruce and lodgepole pine forest
- Wildlife sightings possible — elk, deer, and occasionally moose
- Post-race gathering at the Kananaskis visitor centre
- Part of a broader winter recreation area with extensive trail networks

## Climate

Rocky Mountain continental winter

Late January in the Canadian Rockies brings reliably cold conditions. Temperatures typically -25°C to -10°C. Chinook winds can occasionally bring sudden warming, but deep cold is the norm at this elevation.

Typical race-day temperature: -25 to -10°C

Challenges:
- Extreme cold possible — wind chill below -35°C in exposed sections
- Chinook winds can cause rapid temperature swings
- Short daylight hours in late January at this latitude
- Altitude adjustment needed for flatlanders

## History

A relatively young race established in the mid-2000s to showcase the Kananaskis Country trail system for competitive Nordic skiing. The event draws primarily from Alberta's active Nordic community, particularly Calgary-area clubs. Kananaskis has hosted provincial-level competitions and the marathon builds on the region's history as a training venue for Canadian national team members.

### Notable Facts

- Kananaskis Country hosted events during the 1988 Calgary Winter Olympics
- The trail system is one of the most extensive groomed networks in the Canadian Rockies
- Draws primarily from Calgary and Alberta Nordic clubs
- Elevation gives the course a true mountain skiing character
