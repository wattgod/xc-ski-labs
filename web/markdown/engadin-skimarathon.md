## Course

Point-to-point through the Upper Engadin valley, crossing frozen lakes between Maloja and S-chanf. The course is fast and relatively flat, making it one of the fastest marathon courses in the world. Stunning views of the Bernina massif throughout.

Format: point-to-point  
Surface: Machine-groomed skating lanes on frozen lake surfaces and valley floor  
Technical rating: 2/5  
Grooming: World-class Worldloppet standard on frozen lake surfaces

### Features

- Skating across frozen Engadin lakes — unique in world skiing
- Bernina massif panorama (4,049m) as constant backdrop
- One of the fastest marathon courses due to flat lake crossings
- Passes through St. Moritz — the birthplace of winter tourism
- Feed stations serve Engadin Nusstorte (local nut cake)

## Climate

High alpine valley

At 1,800m altitude, conditions are reliably cold and sunny. The Engadin is famous for over 300 days of sunshine per year. Temperatures typically -10°C to 0°C on race day. Altitude ensures snow reliability but can cause respiratory stress for unacclimatized racers.

Typical race-day temperature: -10 to 0°C

Challenges:
- High altitude (1,800m) affects aerobic performance for unacclimatized skiers
- Intense UV reflection off frozen lakes requires eye protection
- Dry high-altitude air demands extra hydration
- Occasional Maloja wind funnels through the pass

## History

Founded in 1969 with 945 participants, the Engadin Skimarathon has grown to become the largest cross-country skiing event in Switzerland and one of the most iconic freestyle races in the world. The course crosses the frozen Engadin lakes at 1,800m altitude with views of the Bernina massif. The race helped establish the Upper Engadin as a premier cross-country skiing destination alongside its reputation for alpine skiing in St. Moritz.

### Notable Facts

- First edition in 1969 drew 945 participants — now exceeds 13,500
- Crosses frozen lakes including Lake Silvaplana and Lake St. Moritz
- One of the founding members of the Worldloppet series
- St. Moritz hosted the Winter Olympics in 1928 and 1948
- The Engadin valley averages over 300 days of sunshine per year
- Known as the fastest marathon course due to flat lake crossings and altitude-thinned air
