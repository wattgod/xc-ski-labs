## Course

Three-day stage race traversing the fells of western Finnish Lapland from Hetta to Muonio. The course crosses frozen lakes, climbs over treeline fell plateaus, and descends through Arctic birch forests. Each stage averages over 60km with significant cumulative elevation.

Format: stage  
Surface: Machine-groomed tracks through fell wilderness, frozen lakes, and birch forest  
Technical rating: 4/5  
Grooming: Good Finnish standard, prepared for both classic and skate

## Climate

Sub-Arctic Finnish Lapland

April in western Lapland brings lengthening days but still-cold temperatures. Race conditions typically range from -15°C to 0°C. Late-season sun can soften snow dramatically by afternoon, creating challenging wax conditions across the three days.

Typical race-day temperature: -10 to 0°C

Challenges:
- Three consecutive days of ultra-distance skiing
- Dramatic temperature swings between morning and afternoon stages
- Late-season sun creating wet, grabby snow conditions
- Remote fell terrain with limited shelter from wind
- Cumulative fatigue across 190km demands careful pacing

## History

Founded in 1978, Lapponia Hiihto was conceived as a multi-day wilderness crossing that would showcase the vast fell landscape of western Finnish Lapland. The route from Hetta to Muonio traverses some of the most remote terrain in Finland. The race joined the Ski Classics Challengers series, bringing international attention to this unique stage-race format.

### Notable Facts

- At 190km over three days, it is the longest multi-stage XC ski race in Nordic countries
- The route crosses the traditional homeland of the Sámi people
- Hetta (Enontekiö) and Muonio are among Finland's northernmost municipalities
- Part of the Ski Classics Challengers series
- Snow conditions in April Lapland are among the most reliable in the world
- The fell landscape is designated as a UNESCO biosphere reserve area
