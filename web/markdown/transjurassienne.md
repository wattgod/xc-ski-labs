## Course

Point-to-point through the Jura Mountains from Lamoura to Mouthe, traversing the length of the Jura range. The course is relentlessly hilly with over 1,000m of climbing, passing through dense conifer forest. One of the most physically demanding courses on the Worldloppet circuit.

Format: point-to-point  
Surface: Machine-groomed tracks through mountain forest terrain  
Technical rating: 4/5  
Grooming: High-quality grooming, Worldloppet member standard

### Features

- Over 1,000m of climbing makes it one of the hilliest Worldloppet races
- Dense Jura conifer forest creates atmospheric, sheltered skiing
- Offers both classic and skate on the full 68km distance
- Mouthe finish — known as the coldest village in France
- French feed stations with regional cheese, warm wine, and pastries

## Climate

Mid-altitude mountain, continental French

February in the Jura Mountains delivers variable conditions. The moderate altitude (800-1,200m) makes snow reliability the race's biggest challenge — multiple cancellations in recent decades due to insufficient snow. When conditions are good, expect -8°C to 0°C with forest shelter.

Typical race-day temperature: -8 to 0°C

Challenges:
- Snow reliability is the race's Achilles heel — multiple cancellations
- Moderate altitude means rain is possible even in February
- Hilly terrain with 1,000m+ of climbing tests endurance deeply
- Variable conditions within the same race day as altitude changes

## History

Founded in 1979, La Transjurassienne traverses the Jura Mountains in eastern France, covering 68km with over 1,000m of climbing. It is France's premier long-distance ski race and a proud member of the Worldloppet series. The race has faced increasing challenges with snow reliability in recent years due to the moderate altitude of the Jura range, leading to several cancellations and course modifications.

### Notable Facts

- France's premier long-distance cross-country ski race since 1979
- The finish town of Mouthe holds France's cold temperature record (-36.7°C in 1968)
- Has been cancelled or shortened multiple times due to insufficient snow
- The Jura Mountains are the birthplace of French competitive nordic skiing
- Part of the Worldloppet series
- The 1,000m+ of climbing makes it one of the most physically demanding Worldloppet races
