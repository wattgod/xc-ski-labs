## Course

A unique downhill cross-country course starting near the summit of Mount Mansfield (Vermont's highest peak at 1,339m) and descending 823 vertical meters to Stowe village. The course uses a mix of ski trails, logging roads, and backcountry routes. Technical descents dominate — this is not a typical XC marathon but a gravity-assisted adventure through the Green Mountains.

Format: point-to-point  
Surface: Mixed — groomed trails, logging roads, and backcountry routes

## Climate

Northern New England winter

Late February in Vermont's Green Mountains brings variable conditions. Temperatures range from -15°C to 0°C. Mount Mansfield's summit can be Arctic-cold while the valley finish is significantly warmer. Snow cover is generally good at altitude but lower sections can be thin or icy. Wind on exposed mountain sections adds serious chill.

Typical race-day temperature: -15 to 0°C

Challenges:
- Extreme temperature differential between summit start and valley finish
- Ice and hardpack on steep descents
- Wind exposure on upper mountain sections
- Variable snow coverage on lower-elevation trails

## History

Founded in 1945, the Stowe Derby is one of the oldest ski races in the United States. The concept is unique: a 20km descent from near the summit of Mount Mansfield to Stowe village, dropping 2,700 vertical feet. Originally conceived as a test of all-around skiing ability, the Derby predates the modern separation of alpine and Nordic disciplines. It remains a beloved Vermont institution and a rite of passage for adventurous New England skiers.

### Notable Facts

- One of the oldest continuously-run ski races in America — since 1945
- Unique downhill XC format — 2,700 feet of vertical descent
- Predates the modern separation of alpine and Nordic skiing
- Mount Mansfield is Vermont's highest peak at 4,393 feet (1,339m)
- Course uses a mix of ski trails, logging roads, and backcountry routes
- Considered a rite of passage for New England cross-country skiers
