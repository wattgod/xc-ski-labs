## Climate

Great Lakes snow belt winter

February in Michigan's western UP receives massive lake-effect snowfall from Lake Superior. Temperatures typically -18°C to -5°C. The Ironwood area is one of the snowiest locations in the Midwest, regularly exceeding 200 inches of annual snowfall.

Typical race-day temperature: -18 to -5°C

Challenges:
- Deep cold in February with significant wind chill
- Heavy lake-effect snow can dump fresh powder on race day
- Remote UP location requires travel planning
- Trail conditions can vary from packed to fresh dump after a storm

## History

The Sisu Ski Fest celebrates the Finnish-American heritage of Michigan's Upper Peninsula, where Finnish immigrants settled in the late 19th and early 20th centuries to work the iron and copper mines. 'Sisu' is a Finnish concept roughly translating to stoic determination, grit, and stubborn endurance — qualities that define both UP winters and the skiing culture they produced. The festival combines racing with cultural celebration.

### Notable Facts

- 'Sisu' is a uniquely Finnish concept of stubborn endurance and resilience
- Michigan's western UP has one of the largest Finnish-American populations in the US
- Finnish immigrants came to work the UP's iron and copper mines in the 1800s
- The Ironwood area receives over 200 inches of snow annually
- Festival format combines racing with Finnish-American cultural events
- The UP's skiing tradition directly descends from Finnish immigrant culture
