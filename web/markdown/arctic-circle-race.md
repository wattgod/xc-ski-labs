## Course

Three-stage point-to-point race through the Greenland wilderness near Sisimiut. The route crosses frozen fjords, glacial plateaus, and Arctic tundra. Terrain is rugged and variable — windswept ice, deep powder, and machine-tracked sections alternate. Significant elevation gain across the three stages with exposed ridgeline crossings.

Format: stage  
Surface: Mixed — snowmobile-tracked trails, windswept ice, ungroomed wilderness sections  
Technical rating: 5/5  
Grooming: Minimal — snowmobile tracks set before each stage, but conditions vary wildly with wind and weather

## Climate

Arctic maritime/continental

Late March in West Greenland brings extreme cold with temperatures routinely dropping to -25°C or below overnight. Daytime temperatures may hover around -10°C to -15°C. Blizzard conditions are possible. Participants sleep in tents on the snow between stages, exposed to the full force of Arctic weather. Wind chill is the primary hazard.

Typical race-day temperature: -30 to -10°C

Challenges:
- Overnight tent camping at -25°C or colder between stages
- Blizzard risk with whiteout conditions and zero visibility
- Wind chill can push effective temperature below -40°C
- Mandatory survival gear adds weight to racing kit
- Frostbite risk on all exposed skin during three-day effort
- Mental endurance tested by isolation, cold, and multi-day fatigue

## History

The Arctic Circle Race was founded in 1998 in Sisimiut, West Greenland. It was created to showcase Greenland's extraordinary Arctic landscape through competitive skiing. The three-day stage format with tent camping was inspired by Arctic expedition traditions, and the race has earned a reputation as one of the most extreme organized endurance events in the world. Despite its small field of around 200, the race draws participants from over 20 countries — adventure athletes and ultra-endurance specialists who seek the ultimate combination of racing and survival.

### Notable Facts

- Participants sleep in tents at -25°C between stages — no lodges or heated shelters
- Mandatory equipment list includes sleeping bag rated to -30°C, stove, and emergency food
- The course crosses terrain used by Inuit hunters for thousands of years
- Sisimiut is Greenland's second-largest town with about 5,500 residents
- Whiteout conditions have forced stage modifications in several years
- The race attracts special forces soldiers, polar explorers, and ultra-endurance athletes
- Greenland's national ski culture is deeply tied to traditional Inuit winter travel
