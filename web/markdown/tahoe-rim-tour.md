## Course

Loop course at elevation along the Tahoe rim with views of the lake and surrounding Sierra peaks. The course traverses through mixed conifer forest and open ridgelines, with sustained climbs and exhilarating descents. High altitude and rolling terrain make it more demanding than the distance suggests.

Format: loop  
Surface: Machine-groomed trails through Sierra Nevada forest and ridgelines  
Technical rating: 3/5  
Grooming: Well-groomed by local Tahoe Nordic center

## Climate

Sierra Nevada mountain winter

Lake Tahoe in mid-February sees temperatures from -10°C to 5°C. The Sierra Nevada receives massive snowfall from Pacific storms, but the high altitude and California sunshine can create variable snow conditions. Altitude of ~2000m provides excellent snow preservation.

Typical race-day temperature: -10 to 5°C

Challenges:
- Intense sun at altitude can soften snow by midday
- Altitude around 2000m may affect unacclimatized participants
- Sierra storms can dump heavy wet snow
- Variable conditions from sun crust to powder within the same day

## History

The Tahoe Rim Tour emerged from the Lake Tahoe Nordic community, leveraging the Sierra Nevada's massive snowfall and high altitude to create a scenic touring race. The Tahoe area has a long tradition of Nordic skiing, with multiple Nordic centers operating around the lake. The race showcases the unique combination of California sunshine and Sierra powder.

### Notable Facts

- Lake Tahoe area receives some of the heaviest snowfall in the US — often exceeding 10 meters annually
- The Sierra Nevada rim provides a dramatic natural course setting
- Tahoe has been a Nordic skiing destination since the 1960 Winter Olympics at nearby Squaw Valley
- One of the few Nordic races held at approximately 2000m altitude in the US
- The Tahoe Nordic community supports multiple groomed trail systems around the lake
