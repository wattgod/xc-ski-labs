## Course

Point-to-point from Otepää to Elva through the rolling hills and forests of southern Estonia. Despite the low altitude, the terrain has gentle undulation through mixed forest and open farmland. Otepää is known as the 'winter capital' of Estonia.

## Climate

Baltic continental winter

February in southern Estonia delivers cold continental conditions moderated by Baltic proximity. Temperatures typically -15°C to -3°C. Snow reliability has become increasingly variable — the low altitude and Baltic influence mean warm winters can threaten conditions.

Typical race-day temperature: -15 to -3°C

## History

Founded in 1960, the Tartu Maraton is one of the oldest ski marathons in Europe and Estonia's most important winter sporting event. The race connects Otepää — a town with deep ties to Estonian skiing and identity — to Elva through southern Estonian countryside. Despite the country's low altitude and flat terrain, Estonia has a proud nordic skiing tradition, and the Tartu Maraton is its centerpiece. The race is a Worldloppet member.

### Notable Facts

- Founded in 1960 — one of the oldest ski marathons in Europe
- Otepää is Estonia's 'winter capital' and has hosted FIS World Cup events
- Estonia's most important winter mass-participation sporting event
- Part of the Worldloppet series
- The Estonian flag was first consecrated in Otepää in 1884 — the town has deep national significance
- One of the lowest-altitude courses in the Worldloppet series
