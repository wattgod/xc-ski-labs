## Course

Loop course across the Sirdal mountain plateau, traversing open highland terrain with views toward the Ryfylke mountains. The course includes both exposed plateau skiing and sheltered valley sections.

## Climate

Maritime mountain, variable conditions

Sirdal sits on the transition between coastal and mountain climate in southwestern Norway. Atlantic weather systems bring variable conditions. Temperatures range from -10°C to +2°C. Snow quality can shift rapidly from powder to wet depending on weather patterns.

Typical race-day temperature: -8 to +2°C

Challenges:
- Atlantic weather systems create rapid condition changes
- Rain risk even in February at this latitude
- Wind exposure on open plateau sections
- Wax challenges from temperature swings

## History

Founded in 1973 to promote skiing in the Sirdal mountain plateau, one of southwestern Norway's premier winter destinations. The region serves as the closest mountain skiing for the Stavanger and Kristiansand populations. Despite its relatively southern location, the high plateau ensures reliable skiing most winters.

### Notable Facts

- Sirdal is the closest mountain ski area to Stavanger, Norway's oil capital
- The plateau connects to the famous Ryfylke mountain area and Lysebotn
- The region has over 200km of groomed cross-country trails
- Sirdal municipality is home to only ~1,800 permanent residents
