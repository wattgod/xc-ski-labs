## Course

Valley-floor course through the Lesachtal and surrounding Alpine terrain at 1,400m. The route follows the Gail River valley with views of the Carnic Alps and Dolomite foothills. Moderate elevation changes with some rolling hills, predominantly through open meadows and forest.

Format: loop  
Surface: Machine-groomed trails at high altitude through Alpine valley and forest  
Technical rating: 3/5  
Grooming: Excellent Austrian grooming standards at a dedicated biathlon and cross-country venue

### Features

- Course at 1,400m altitude — one of the highest Worldloppet races

## Climate

High-altitude Alpine

January at 1,400m in East Tyrol delivers reliable cold and deep snow. The Gailtal Alps create a snow-pocket effect, and the high altitude ensures consistently cold temperatures. Clear skies reveal the Dolomite peaks; heavy snowfall years produce exceptional trail conditions.

Typical race-day temperature: -15 to -3°C

Challenges:
- High altitude (1,400m) demands acclimatization for lowland skiers
- January cold can be extreme — sub-minus-20 not uncommon
- Alpine valley inversions trap cold air, creating bitter conditions at the start
- Remote location requires planning for travel and accommodation

## History

The Dolomitenlauf was founded in 1970 in Obertilliach, a small village in Austria's East Tyrol region. The high-altitude venue at 1,400m was chosen for its exceptional snow reliability, and the village has since developed into a dedicated biathlon and cross-country skiing center. The race joined the Worldloppet series and has become a fixture for skiers seeking guaranteed snow conditions and dramatic Alpine scenery early in the season.

### Notable Facts

- One of the highest-altitude races in the Worldloppet circuit at 1,400m
- Obertilliach serves as a training base for Austrian biathlon teams
- The Lesachtal valley is UNESCO-recognized as one of Europe's most pristine Alpine valleys
- January timing makes it one of the earliest major XC ski races each season
- East Tyrol was historically part of the Austro-Hungarian frontier with Italy
- The Dolomites are a UNESCO World Heritage Site
