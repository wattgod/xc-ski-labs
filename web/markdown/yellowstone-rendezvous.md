## Course

A rolling loop course on the Rendezvous Ski Trails just outside Yellowstone National Park at 6,600 feet. The terrain is gently rolling through lodgepole pine forest with moderate climbs. The trail system is renowned for its consistent grooming and deep snowpack, serving as a US Biathlon team training venue. Views of the surrounding Madison Range and Yellowstone plateau.

Format: loop  
Surface: Machine-groomed on the Rendezvous Ski Trail system  
Technical rating: 2/5

## Climate

High-altitude Rocky Mountain winter

March at 6,600 feet (2,012m) near Yellowstone means cold, dry conditions with deep snowpack. Temperatures range from -18°C to -2°C. West Yellowstone is one of the coldest towns in the contiguous US. March brings longer days and spring sun but temperatures remain well below freezing, especially at night. Snow is virtually guaranteed.

Typical race-day temperature: -18 to -2°C

Challenges:

## History

Founded in 1981, the Yellowstone Rendezvous has become one of the premier ski marathons in the American West. Set on the Rendezvous Ski Trails just outside Yellowstone National Park, the race benefits from West Yellowstone's legendary cold and deep snowpack. The trail system also serves as a training venue for the US Biathlon team, ensuring professional-grade grooming. The name honors the historic fur trade rendezvous gatherings that once took place in the region.

### Notable Facts

- Adjacent to Yellowstone — the world's first national park (established 1872)
- Rendezvous Ski Trails serve as a US Biathlon team training venue
- West Yellowstone is consistently one of the coldest towns in the contiguous US
- Founded in 1981 — over 45 years of continuous racing
- Named after historic fur trade rendezvous gatherings in the region
- 6,600 feet altitude with some of the deepest snowpack in Montana
