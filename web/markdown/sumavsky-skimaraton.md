## Course

Starting from Kvilda, one of the highest villages in the Czech Republic, the course traverses the Šumava National Park plateau. The terrain features rolling mountain forest with open moorland sections. Šumava (Bohemian Forest) contains some of Europe's last primeval forests and is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve.

Format: loop  
Surface: Machine-groomed freestyle tracks through national park terrain

## Climate

Central European mountain winter

February at 1,000m+ in the Šumava mountains means reliable winter conditions. The Bohemian Forest is one of the snowiest regions in Central Europe. Temperatures typically -8°C to -1°C. Being further south than Scandinavian races, conditions can be more variable.

Typical race-day temperature: -8 to -1°C

Challenges:
- Central European snow reliability less certain than Scandinavia
- Altitude-dependent conditions — lower sections may be marginal
- Wind on exposed mountain plateau sections
- National park terrain includes some challenging descents

## History

Founded in 1984, the Šumavský Skimaraton is a 46km freestyle race through Šumava National Park in the Bohemian Forest of South Bohemia, Czech Republic. Part of the Euroloppet series, it starts from Kvilda, one of the highest and coldest villages in the country. The Šumava range straddles the Czech-German-Austrian border and contains some of Europe's last remaining primeval forest.

### Notable Facts

- Šumava National Park is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve
- Kvilda is one of the coldest places in the Czech Republic — extreme temperature records below -40°C
- The Bohemian Forest (Šumava/Böhmerwald) spans Czech, German, and Austrian territory
- Contains some of Europe's last primeval old-growth forest
- Part of the Euroloppet series
- Founded in 1984, over 40 years of racing history
