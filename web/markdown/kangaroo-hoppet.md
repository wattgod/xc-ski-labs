## Course

High-altitude course through the Falls Creek alpine plateau at 1,600m. The route traverses snow-gum woodland and open alpine terrain with views across the Victorian Alps. Rolling terrain with some significant climbs. The unique Australian alpine landscape — snow gums, granite boulders, and high plains — is unlike any other Worldloppet venue.

Format: loop  
Surface: Machine-groomed trails on alpine plateau, variable snow quality  
Technical rating: 3/5  
Grooming: Good quality given conditions — Falls Creek maintains Australia's best XC trail network

## Climate

Australian alpine winter

August is late winter in the Australian Alps. Conditions at 1,600m are variable — snowfall, rain, fog, and sunshine can all occur within hours. Snow cover is less reliable than Northern Hemisphere alpine venues, and warm fronts from the Tasman Sea can bring rapid thawing. When conditions align, the snow-gum-dotted landscape is uniquely beautiful.

Typical race-day temperature: -5 to 5°C

Challenges:
- Snow reliability is the key risk — warm years can force course modification or cancellation
- Rapidly changing weather as Southern Ocean fronts sweep through
- High altitude with UV exposure — the Australian sun is intense even in winter
- Variable snow texture from freeze-thaw cycles common in Australian Alps

## History

The Kangaroo Hoppet was founded in 1991 (with a precursor event in 1979) at Falls Creek Alpine Resort in the Victorian Alps of southeastern Australia. It holds the unique distinction of being the only Worldloppet race in the Southern Hemisphere, making it a bucket-list event for Worldloppet passport completionists. The race celebrates Australia's small but passionate cross-country skiing community and offers Northern Hemisphere skiers a rare opportunity to race during their off-season. Snow reliability has been an ongoing challenge, with several cancellations due to insufficient snow.
