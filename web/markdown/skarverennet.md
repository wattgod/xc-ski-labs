## Course

Point-to-point across the Hardangervidda mountain plateau, the largest high-altitude plateau in Northern Europe. The course is fully exposed above treeline with dramatic mountain scenery. Undulating terrain with no shelter from wind.

## Climate

High mountain plateau, spring conditions

Late April on the Hardangervidda means spring skiing with longer days but still winter conditions at altitude. Temperatures typically -5°C to +5°C. The open plateau is notoriously exposed to wind — severe weather can force cancellation or course changes.

Typical race-day temperature: -5 to +5°C

Challenges:
- Extreme wind exposure on the open Hardangervidda plateau
- April weather can shift rapidly between winter and spring conditions
- Whiteout conditions possible in storms
- Sun-softened snow in afternoon warmth affects wax and glide
- The course has been cancelled or shortened due to extreme wind

## History

Founded in 1974, Skarverennet has grown to become Norway's largest ski race by participant count, with capacity for over 12,000 skiers. The race crosses the Hardangervidda, Europe's largest mountain plateau, on a course that is entirely above treeline. The late April date takes advantage of reliable spring snow at altitude and provides a dramatic end to the Norwegian ski season. The name references the mountain Skarven near the course.

### Notable Facts

- Norway's largest ski race by participant count — capacity over 12,000
- Crosses the Hardangervidda, Northern Europe's largest mountain plateau
- Late April date makes it one of the last major ski races of the season
- Entirely above treeline — one of the most exposed courses in world skiing
- Near Finse, where Roald Amundsen trained for his South Pole expedition
- The Bergen Railway provides access — Finse is Norway's highest railway station
