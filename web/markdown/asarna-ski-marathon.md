## Course

90km point-to-point course through the forests and rolling hills of Jämtland. One of Sweden's longest ski marathons, the route traverses classic Scandinavian wilderness terrain with sustained climbs and fast descents through boreal forest.

## Climate

Inland Scandinavian continental

Jämtland's inland position produces reliable cold and dry winter conditions. February races benefit from deep snowpack and consistent sub-zero temperatures, though extreme cold snaps below -25°C can occur.

Typical race-day temperature: -20 to -5°C

Challenges:
- Extreme cold in February — temperatures can drop below -25°C
- Long exposure time on 90km course amplifies cold risk
- Wax management critical in wide temperature swings
- Remote terrain with limited shelter between stations

## History

The Åsarna Ski Marathon has been a fixture of Swedish long-distance skiing since 1940. Based in the small village of Åsarna in Jämtland, one of Sweden's premier skiing regions, it offers one of the longest courses in the country at 90km. The race attracts a dedicated following of endurance skiers seeking a genuine test in true winter conditions.

### Notable Facts

- Founded in 1940, making it one of Sweden's oldest marathon ski races
- At 90km, it matches Vasaloppet as one of Sweden's longest races
- Based in Jämtland, one of Sweden's most reliable snow regions
- Attracts a core following of ultra-distance Nordic specialists
