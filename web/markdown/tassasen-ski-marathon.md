## Course

100km through the mountains and forests of Härjedalen, one of Sweden's most sparsely populated regions. The course includes significant climbing across plateau terrain and descents through birch forest, demanding both endurance and technical skill over an ultra-marathon distance.

Format: point-to-point  
Surface: Machine-groomed classic tracks through mountain and forest terrain  
Technical rating: 3/5

## Climate

Inland Scandinavian mountain climate

Härjedalen sits between the coastal lowlands and the Norwegian border mountains. Cold and dry conditions are the norm in March, with reliable snowpack at altitude. Wind exposure on open plateau sections adds challenge.

Typical race-day temperature: -18 to -5°C

Challenges:
- Extreme cold and wind on exposed mountain plateau sections
- 100km distance demands meticulous nutrition and energy management
- Remote terrain with long gaps between aid stations
- Late winter daylight still limited at this latitude

## History

The Tåssåsen Ski Marathon is one of Sweden's most extreme ultra-distance ski races, covering 100km through the remote mountains of Härjedalen. Dating from around 1960, it has remained a niche but respected event for skiers seeking distances beyond the standard marathon format. Härjedalen's sparse population and reliable winter conditions make it an authentic wilderness racing experience.

### Notable Facts

- At 100km, one of Sweden's longest ski races
- Based in Härjedalen, one of Sweden's least populated regions
- Reliable snow conditions due to inland mountain location
- Attracts ultra-endurance specialists seeking maximum distance
