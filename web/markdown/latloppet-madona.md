## Climate

Baltic continental winter

Madona sits in the Vidzeme uplands, the hilliest region of Latvia, earning its reputation as the 'winter capital.' The continental influence from the east delivers cold, stable winters. Temperatures range from -15°C to -3°C in February-March. Snow cover is generally reliable but can be threatened by Atlantic warming events.

Typical race-day temperature: -12 to -3°C

Challenges:
- Atlantic warming events can threaten snow cover in mild winters
- Wind exposure on open upland sections
- Variable Baltic weather — conditions can change rapidly
- Cold continental air from Russia can bring extreme temperatures

## History

Founded in 2018, LatLoppet Madona is Latvia's bid to establish a major Nordic ski marathon. Held in Madona, a town in the Vidzeme uplands region that bills itself as the 'winter capital of Latvia,' the race benefits from the area's reliable snow and relatively hilly terrain. The race gained significant credibility by joining the Ski Classics Challengers series, which brings professional skiers and international attention. Though young, the event has grown quickly, drawing over 1,200 participants and establishing Latvia as a player in the European long-distance skiing calendar.

### Notable Facts

- Part of the Ski Classics Challengers series — the pathway to full Ski Classics events
- Madona is known as the 'winter capital of Latvia' for its reliable snow
- The Vidzeme uplands are Latvia's hilliest region — unusual for this generally flat country
- Latvia has a growing Nordic skiing culture, with strong participation in Baltic championships
- Founded in 2018, making it one of the youngest Ski Classics-affiliated events
