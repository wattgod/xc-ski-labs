## Course

Point-to-point through the flat Engadin valley floor from Maloja toward St. Moritz. The course follows a portion of the famous Engadin Skimarathon route along frozen lakes and through the stunning Upper Engadin landscape. Predominantly flat with gentle undulations — the altitude rather than terrain provides the challenge.

Format: point-to-point  
Surface: Superbly groomed valley floor trails at 1800m altitude  
Technical rating: 1/5  
Grooming: Swiss precision grooming — among the best in the world

## Climate

High Alpine valley winter

The Engadin valley at 1800m in early March has excellent snow conditions. Temperatures range from -15°C to 0°C. The high altitude and inner-Alpine position provide reliable cold dry snow. The Engadin is one of the sunniest valleys in the Alps, offering bright racing conditions.

Typical race-day temperature: -15 to 0°C

Challenges:
- High altitude at 1800m affects performance for unacclimatized racers
- Intense Alpine sun can cause rapid snow surface changes
- Cold morning starts with significant temperature rise by finish
- Occasional Maloja wind funneling through the valley

## History

The Engadin Frauenlauf (Engadin Women's Race) was created in 2001 as a companion event to the famous Engadin Skimarathon. It provides a dedicated women-only Nordic racing event in one of the world's most spectacular skiing settings. The race has grown to attract approximately 2,000 women and has become an important event for promoting female participation in Nordic skiing across the Alpine region.

### Notable Facts

- One of the largest women-only cross-country ski races in the world
- Part of the Engadin Skimarathon weekend — one of the world's most prestigious Nordic events
- The Engadin valley has hosted Nordic skiing since the 19th century
- Swiss precision organization and grooming standards
- St. Moritz hosted the Winter Olympics in 1928 and 1948
- The women-only format was pioneering for Nordic skiing events in Switzerland
