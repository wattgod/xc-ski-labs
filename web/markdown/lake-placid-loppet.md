## Course

The most vertically challenging marathon ski race in the eastern US. The course traverses Olympic-era trails around Lake Placid with 1,120 meters of cumulative climbing through dense Adirondack forest, punishing uphills, and technical descents.

Format: loop  
Surface: Machine-groomed Olympic legacy trails through Adirondack forest  
Technical rating: 4/5  
Grooming: Olympic venue standard grooming maintained by the ORDA trail crew

### Features

- 1980 Winter Olympic cross-country ski venue
- 1,120m of climbing — the hilliest marathon ski in the East
- Technical descents through dense Adirondack forest
- Panoramic views of the High Peaks from ridgeline sections
- Classic Olympic village start and finish atmosphere

## Climate

Adirondack mountain winter

Lake Placid's mountain setting delivers cold, snowy winters. January temperatures range from -20°C to -5°C. The Adirondacks receive heavy snowfall, and the Olympic venue's elevation ensures reliable natural coverage. Wind exposure at higher elevations adds a chill factor.

Typical race-day temperature: -20 to -5°C

Challenges:
- Bitter Adirondack cold, especially at higher elevations on the course
- Significant climbing means wide effort swings and sweat management
- Wind exposure on ridgeline sections
- Snow conditions vary with altitude — icy at top, softer in valleys

## History

Founded around 1982, the Lake Placid Loppet capitalizes on the cross-country ski infrastructure built for the 1980 Winter Olympics. The race traverses trails that once hosted Olympic competition, making it one of the few American citizen races on genuine Olympic-legacy terrain. With over 1,100 meters of climbing, it is widely regarded as the most physically demanding marathon ski race in the eastern United States.

### Notable Facts

- Uses trails built for the 1980 Lake Placid Winter Olympics
- 1,120m of climbing makes it the hilliest eastern US ski marathon
- Lake Placid is one of only three US cities to host the Winter Olympics (1932, 1980)
- The Olympic Regional Development Authority (ORDA) maintains the trail system year-round
- Attracts serious endurance skiers seeking mountainous terrain rarely found east of the Rockies
- Multiple technique options — classic, freestyle, or any — reflect its inclusive ethos
