## Course

Course through the Alutaguse wilderness in Ida-Virumaa, Estonia's easternmost region. The terrain crosses raised bogs, pine forest, and spruce woodland in one of the most remote areas of the Baltic states. Flat to gently rolling — classic Estonian geography but in a wilder, less developed setting than other Estoloppet venues.

## Climate

Baltic continental winter

February in Ida-Virumaa brings some of Estonia's most reliable winter conditions. The northeastern location, close to the Russian border, has a more continental climate than western Estonia. Temperatures typically -12°C to -3°C. Better snow reliability than coastal areas.

Typical race-day temperature: -12 to -3°C

Challenges:
- Remote location in eastern Estonia limits access options
- Flat bogland terrain demands sustained effort with no hills for variety
- Baltic snow reliability still less certain than Scandinavian venues
- Frozen bog can create exposed, windy sections

## History

The Alutaguse Maraton is the flagship event of the Estoloppet series, drawing the largest field of any Estonian ski marathon. Held in the Alutaguse wilderness of Ida-Virumaa — Estonia's easternmost region near the Russian border — the race traverses some of the most remote and wild landscape in the Baltic states. The area's raised bogs, ancient spruce forests, and minimal human development create a genuine wilderness skiing experience.

### Notable Facts

- Largest field in the Estoloppet series at approximately 500 participants
- Alutaguse is Estonia's largest wilderness area
- Ida-Virumaa borders Russia — the easternmost point of the EU
- Estonian raised bogs can be thousands of years old
- Part of the Estoloppet series — Estonia's national ski marathon circuit
- The region's continental climate provides better snow than coastal Estonia
