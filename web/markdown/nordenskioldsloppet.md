## Climate

Subarctic continental

Temperatures range from -25°C to -5°C in mid-March above the Arctic Circle. Clear nights bring extreme cold, while daytime sun can soften snow. Skiers race through the full Arctic light cycle — starting in darkness, skiing through dawn, daylight, and potentially back into darkness.

Typical race-day temperature: -20 to -5°C

Challenges:
- Extreme cold during night sections, frostbite risk on exposed skin
- 30-hour effort demands sleep management and caloric planning
- Wax selection across massive temperature swings between night and day
- Arctic wind exposure on open frozen lakes and marshland
- Limited daylight hours — headlamp skiing required for slower participants

## History

Founded in 1884, Nordenskiöldsloppet is the world's longest cross-country ski race and one of the oldest competitive ski events still held. Named after Finnish-Swedish Arctic explorer Adolf Erik Nordenskiöld, the race commemorates the extreme skiing feats of Sami reindeer herders in Swedish Lapland. The event was sporadic in its early decades but has been held consistently in recent years, drawing adventurers and ultra-endurance athletes to Jokkmokk, a Sami cultural center above the Arctic Circle.

### Notable Facts

- Founded in 1884 — predates even the Holmenkollen ski festival
- Named after Adolf Erik Nordenskiöld, the first to navigate the Northeast Passage
- At 220km, it is the longest cross-country ski race in the world
- Jokkmokk hosts the famous Sami winter market, held since 1605
- The 30-hour time limit means some skiers race through an entire Arctic night
- Historically inspired by Sami reindeer herders' legendary long-distance skiing abilities
