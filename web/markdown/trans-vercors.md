## Course

Point-to-point traversal of the Vercors Massif from Saint-Agnan-en-Vercors to Villard-de-Lans. Massive elevation profile with 1,140m of positive gain across 53km. The course crosses multiple cols and plateaus through one of France's most dramatic limestone massifs. Technical descents and sustained climbs define the experience.

Format: point-to-point  
Surface: Machine-groomed trails across the Vercors Nordic domain  
Technical rating: 4/5  
Grooming: Good French Nordic grooming — variable on remote plateau sections

## Climate

Pre-Alpine Vercors plateau winter

February on the Vercors Massif delivers genuine Alpine winter conditions. Temperatures range from -10°C to 2°C. The Vercors is known for reliable snowfall and cold temperatures thanks to its plateau geography. North-facing slopes hold snow well, but exposed ridgeline sections can be windswept.

Typical race-day temperature: -10 to 2°C

Challenges:
- 1,140m of positive elevation gain makes this a serious climbing race
- Exposed plateau and ridgeline sections with wind chill
- Variable snow conditions across different altitudes and aspects
- Descents require strong technical skill at speed
- Race has been cancelled in extreme weather years

## History

The Trans'Vercors is one of France's oldest cross-country ski marathons, first held in 1968. The race traverses the Vercors Massif, a dramatic limestone plateau in the French Pre-Alps that served as a major stronghold for the French Resistance during World War II. The massif's unique geography — high plateaus ringed by steep cliffs — creates a natural fortress that sheltered resistance fighters and now provides an extraordinary skiing landscape. With 1,140m of elevation gain, it ranks among the most demanding XC marathons in Europe.

### Notable Facts

- Founded in 1968 — one of France's oldest XC ski marathons
- 1,140m of positive elevation gain across 53km
- The Vercors Massif was a WWII Resistance stronghold — the Maquis du Vercors
- Dramatic limestone plateau and cliff scenery
- Point-to-point traversal finishing in the resort village of Villard-de-Lans
- Grenoble (1968 Winter Olympics host city) is the nearest major city
