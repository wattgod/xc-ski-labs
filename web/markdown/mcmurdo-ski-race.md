## Course

Out-and-back course on the Ross Ice Shelf and sea ice near McMurdo Station, with views of Mount Erebus — the southernmost active volcano — and the Transantarctic Mountains. Flat, windswept terrain on packed snow and sea ice. The course is surveyed for crevasse safety before each race.

Format: out-and-back  
Surface: Wind-packed snow and sea ice, flagged course with no grooming  
Technical rating: 2/5  
Grooming: No grooming — natural wind-packed surface, flagged route only

### Features

- World's southernmost cross-country ski race at 77°S
- Views of Mount Erebus, the southernmost active volcano on Earth
- Course surveyed for crevasse safety before each edition
- 24-hour daylight in October — no headlamps needed
- Participants are Antarctic research station personnel — scientists, support staff, and military

## Climate

Antarctic polar

McMurdo Station sits on Ross Island at 77°S. October is early austral spring with 24-hour daylight returning. Temperatures range from -30°C to -15°C. Katabatic winds descending from the Transantarctic Mountains can reach 100+ km/h without warning. Wind chill can push effective temperatures below -50°C. Snow is dry, wind-packed, and crystalline.

Typical race-day temperature: -25 to -15°C

Challenges:
- Katabatic winds can appear without warning — race may be postponed or cancelled
- Extreme cold with wind chill below -50°C requires full face protection
- Altitude equivalent effect — low barometric pressure at high latitude mimics ~1,000m altitude
- No rescue infrastructure beyond station medical facilities
- UV radiation extreme with 24-hour daylight and ozone-depleted atmosphere

## History

Organized informally since the early 2000s by outdoor recreation staff at McMurdo Station, the United States Antarctic Program's main research base. The race began as a morale event for winterover personnel and has grown to include summer-season staff from McMurdo and neighboring New Zealand's Scott Base. Participants are scientists, engineers, pilots, cooks, and support staff — no tourists. The race represents the extreme frontier of Nordic skiing, held on Ross Island where Scott and Shackleton launched their legendary expeditions.

### Notable Facts

- McMurdo Station is the largest research station in Antarctica with ~1,000 summer residents
- The race is held near the historic huts of Scott (1902) and Shackleton (1908)
- Mount Erebus (3,794m) — the southernmost active volcano — dominates the course backdrop
- Only accessible by military or research aircraft — no commercial flights to McMurdo
- The Ross Ice Shelf adjacent to the course is the world's largest floating ice mass
- Multi-national field — personnel from the US, New Zealand, Italy, and other Antarctic Treaty nations
