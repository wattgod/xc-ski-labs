## Course

A 42km course through the Niseko valley beneath Mount Yōtei's perfect volcanic cone. The route passes through birch forest and open farmland with the iconic Yōtei-zan backdrop. Japan's deepest snowpack means the course sits atop meters of compacted snow. Impeccable Japanese grooming standards.

Format: loop

## Climate

Sea of Japan snow belt

Niseko receives some of the world's heaviest snowfall — over 15 meters per season. The Sea of Japan effect creates continuous powder dumps from November through April. Temperatures range from -15°C to -2°C. Snow is so reliable it's essentially guaranteed.

Typical race-day temperature: -15 to -2°C

Challenges:
- Extremely heavy snowfall can bury the course overnight
- Low visibility during frequent snow squalls
- Deep powder adjacent to groomed tracks makes recovery from falls difficult
- Course preparation is a constant battle against fresh snowfall

## History

Founded in 1982 as Hokkaido's premier citizen XC ski marathon, the Niseko Ski Marathon leverages the region's legendary snowfall and Mount Yōtei's stunning volcanic scenery. Niseko has become internationally famous for powder skiing since the 2000s, and the XC marathon benefits from the area's growing international winter sports community. Japan has a deep XC skiing tradition in Hokkaido.

### Notable Facts

- Niseko receives over 15 meters of snowfall annually — among the deepest in the world
- Mount Yōtei (1898m) is called 'Ezo Fuji' for its resemblance to Mount Fuji
- Hokkaido has hosted XC ski events since the 1930s
- Niseko's international community has grown the event's non-Japanese participation
- Japan's XC ski marathon culture is well-established with over a dozen annual events
