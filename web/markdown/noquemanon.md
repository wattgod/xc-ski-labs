## Climate

Great Lakes snow belt winter

Michigan's Upper Peninsula receives massive lake-effect snowfall from Lake Superior, averaging over 200 inches per season. January temperatures range from -20°C to -5°C. The lake-effect snow machine makes this one of the snowiest regions in the eastern United States.

Typical race-day temperature: -18 to -5°C

Challenges:
- Heavy lake-effect snowfall can dump fresh snow on race day
- Deep cold in late January with wind chill factor
- Trail conditions can vary from packed powder to fresh dump
- Iron ore country terrain with rolling, wooded hills

## History

Founded in 2001, the Noquemanon Ski Marathon takes its name from a Chippewa word meaning 'bear' — fitting for a race through the rugged wilderness of Michigan's Upper Peninsula. The event was created to showcase the exceptional Nordic skiing conditions created by Lake Superior's snow belt and to connect the historic mining towns of Ishpeming and Marquette.

### Notable Facts

- Name derives from Chippewa word 'noquemanon' meaning 'bear'
- Michigan's Upper Peninsula receives over 200 inches of snow annually from lake effect
- Ishpeming is home to the US National Ski Hall of Fame
- The UP was historically iron and copper mining country
- Part of the 80+ mile Noquemanon Trail Network
- Marquette finish overlooks Lake Superior, the world's largest freshwater lake by surface area
