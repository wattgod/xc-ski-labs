## Course

Point-to-point through the Northwoods forest from Cable to Hayward on the American Birkebeiner Ski Trail. Rolling terrain with significant hills — the course is hillier than many expect. The famous finish on Main Street Hayward is one of skiing's great spectacles.

Format: point-to-point  
Surface: Machine-groomed trails through hardwood and pine forest  
Technical rating: 3/5  
Grooming: High-quality machine grooming, well-maintained American Birkebeiner trail system

### Features

- Iconic finish on Main Street Hayward with massive crowds
- Offers both classic and skate distances on separate courses
- The 'Birkie' trail system is groomed year-round for training
- Rolling hills through Northwoods hardwood forest
- Post-race festival in Hayward with live music and community celebration

## History

Founded in 1973 by Tony Wise, inspired by Norway's Birkebeinerrennet. The first race had 35 skiers; it has grown to over 10,000 participants, making it the largest cross-country ski race in North America. The race follows the American Birkebeiner Ski Trail through the Northwoods of Wisconsin. The Birkie has become a cultural institution in the Upper Midwest and a gateway event that introduces thousands of Americans to competitive skiing.

### Notable Facts

- First race in 1973 had just 35 skiers — now North America's largest
- Inspired by Norway's Birkebeinerrennet (founded 1932)
- The Main Street Hayward finish draws thousands of spectators
- The Birkie trail system is maintained year-round and used for mountain biking in summer
- Part of the Worldloppet series
- Has been shortened or rerouted several times due to insufficient snow or extreme cold
