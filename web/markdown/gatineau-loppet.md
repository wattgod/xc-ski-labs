## Course

Hilly course through Gatineau Park, a 361 km² wilderness area across the river from Ottawa. The route traverses the Gatineau Hills with significant climbing through mixed hardwood and boreal forest. Rolling terrain with punchy climbs and fast descents demands strong technique.

Format: point-to-point  
Surface: Machine-groomed trails through Gatineau Park's extensive trail network  
Technical rating: 4/5  
Grooming: Excellent — Gatineau Park maintains one of North America's best groomed trail systems

- Course through Gatineau Park, a massive protected wilderness area

## Climate

Canadian continental winter

February in the Ottawa-Gatineau region brings reliable cold and snow. The Canadian Shield terrain and northern latitude ensure consistent winter conditions. Temperatures are typically well below freezing, with occasional Arctic air masses pushing conditions to extreme cold.

Typical race-day temperature: -18 to -5°C

Challenges:
- Extreme cold when Arctic air masses descend — wind chill can reach -35°C
- Hilly terrain in Gatineau Park exposes skiers to wind on ridgelines
- Variable snow conditions between sheltered forest and open ridges
- Cold-weather gear management critical for the 3-5 hour effort

## History

The Gatineau Loppet was founded in 1979 and quickly became Canada's premier marathon cross-country ski race. Set in Gatineau Park — a vast wilderness preserve just across the Ottawa River from Canada's Parliament — the race benefits from world-class trail infrastructure and a passionate bilingual skiing community. It joined the Worldloppet series and has been North America's most consistent representative on the circuit, drawing skiers from across Canada and the northeastern United States.

### Notable Facts

- Gatineau Park covers 361 km² and maintains over 200km of groomed XC trails
- The park is managed by the National Capital Commission, ensuring top-tier maintenance
- Located just 15 minutes from downtown Ottawa, Canada's capital
- Canadian XC skiing legends Pierre Harvey and Beckie Scott trained on these trails
- The Gatineau Hills are part of the Canadian Shield, one of Earth's oldest geological formations
- Bilingual French-English event reflects Canada's dual linguistic heritage
