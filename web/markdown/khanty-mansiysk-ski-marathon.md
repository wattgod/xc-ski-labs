## Course

A 50km course centered on the Khanty-Mansiysk biathlon center, which has hosted IBU Biathlon World Cup finals. The route extends into the taiga forest along the Irtysh River, passing through birch and pine forest typical of the western Siberian lowlands. Flat to gently rolling terrain on impeccably groomed World Cup trails.

Format: loop  
Surface: Machine-groomed World Cup standard trails through taiga forest  
Technical rating: 2/5  
Grooming: World Cup biathlon venue standard — among Russia's best

## Climate

Continental subarctic winter

Khanty-Mansiysk's Siberian location guarantees deep cold and reliable snow from October through May. March race date means lengthening days but still solid winter conditions. Temperatures range from -25°C to -5°C. Snow depth regularly exceeds 1 meter.

Typical race-day temperature: -25 to -5°C

Challenges:
- Extreme cold possible even in late March
- Flat terrain offers no wind shelter
- Fog from the Irtysh and Ob river confluence
- Frostbite risk in Siberian wind chill conditions

## History

Founded in 1997 as the oil-rich Yugra region invested heavily in winter sports, the Khanty-Mansiysk Ski Marathon uses trails from the IBU Biathlon World Cup venue. The city became a major international biathlon destination in the 2000s, hosting World Cup finals and World Championships. The citizen marathon leverages this infrastructure for one of Siberia's largest ski events.

### Notable Facts

- Khanty-Mansiysk has hosted IBU Biathlon World Cup finals since 2000
- The Yugra region produces much of Russia's oil wealth
- The indigenous Khanty and Mansi peoples have a centuries-old skiing tradition
- The city invested billions of rubles in winter sports infrastructure
- Located at the confluence of the Irtysh and Ob — two of Asia's great rivers
