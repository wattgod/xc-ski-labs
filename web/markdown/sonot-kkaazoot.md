## Climate

Interior subarctic winter

Fairbanks in March delivers the tail end of one of North America's coldest winters. Temperatures can still reach -25°C, though late March may warm to -5°C. The race name — Lower Tanana Athabaskan for 'sliding across snow in springtime' — reflects the seasonal transition. Daylight is rapidly increasing, with 12+ hours by late March.

Typical race-day temperature: -25 to -5°C

Challenges:
- Extreme cold possible — Fairbanks regularly sees -30°C in winter
- Ice fog in deep cold obscures visibility
- Remote location with limited emergency access on course
- Wax selection for extremely cold, dry Interior Alaska snow

## History

Founded in 1988, the Sonot Kkaazoot takes its name from the Lower Tanana Athabaskan phrase meaning 'sliding across snow in springtime.' The race celebrates Fairbanks' deep connection to Nordic skiing — in a city where temperatures routinely hit -40°C, cross-country skiing is both transportation and survival skill. Despite its small field, the Sonot Kkaazoot embodies the spirit of interior Alaska: tough, remote, and profoundly connected to the land.

### Notable Facts

- Name means 'sliding across snow in springtime' in Lower Tanana Athabaskan
- One of the northernmost marathon ski races in North America at 64°N latitude
- Fairbanks is one of the coldest cities in the US — winter lows reach -50°C
- The University of Alaska Fairbanks has a strong Nordic ski program
- Late March timing allows 12+ hours of daylight versus the near-darkness of December
- Small field size — typically around 200 — gives it a backcountry expedition feel
