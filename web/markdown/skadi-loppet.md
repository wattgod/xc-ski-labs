## Course

Loop course through the Bavarian Forest with a massive 1,077 meters of total ascent — making it one of the most demanding marathon ski races in central Europe. The course climbs through dense spruce and beech forest to exposed ridgelines before plunging back down through tight, technical descents. The terrain is relentless.

Format: loop  
Surface: Machine-groomed trails through dense mountain forest and ridgeline terrain  
Technical rating: 5/5  
Grooming: Good German-standard grooming, adapted to demanding terrain

## History

Founded in 2000, the Skadi Loppet takes its name from the Norse goddess Skadi, associated with winter, mountains, and skiing. The race was created to showcase the Bavarian Forest's exceptional Nordic skiing terrain and has earned a reputation as one of the most physically demanding marathon ski races in central Europe. Its 1,077m of climbing in just 42km is nearly unmatched.

### Notable Facts

- Named after Skadi, the Norse goddess of winter, mountains, and skiing
- 1,077m of total ascent makes it one of Europe's toughest marathon ski races
- The Bavarian Forest is Germany's first national park (established 1970)
- Nearly 100% snow reliability — the Bodenmais area is one of Germany's snowiest
- Euroloppet member connecting it to the European marathon skiing circuit
- The Bavarian Forest borders Sumava National Park in the Czech Republic
