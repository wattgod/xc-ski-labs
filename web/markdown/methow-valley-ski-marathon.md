## Course

Point-to-point through the Methow Valley trail system, one of the largest groomed Nordic trail networks in North America. The course follows the valley floor and surrounding terrain through open meadows and pine forests with stunning views of the North Cascades. Rolling terrain with sustained climbs.

## Climate

Pacific Northwest mountain winter

The Methow Valley sits in the rain shadow of the North Cascades, producing cold dry winters with reliable snowfall. February temperatures typically range from -15°C to 0°C. The valley's eastside location gives it more continental-style cold and dry snow than the wet western slopes.

Typical race-day temperature: -15 to 0°C

Challenges:
- Occasional warm Chinook winds can soften snow rapidly
- Cold morning starts with significant temperature swings by afternoon
- Dry air and sun at altitude can cause dehydration
- Remote location means weather changes can be impactful

## History

The Methow Valley Ski Marathon takes advantage of the Methow Valley Sport Trails Association (MVSTA) trail network, one of the largest groomed Nordic systems in North America with over 200km of trails. Founded in 1993, the race showcases the dry, cold snow and spectacular mountain scenery of the North Cascades rain shadow. The Methow Valley has become one of the premier Nordic destinations in the Pacific Northwest.

### Notable Facts

- The MVSTA maintains over 200km of groomed Nordic trails
- The Methow Valley sits in the rain shadow of the North Cascades, producing dry powder snow
- Winthrop is a restored Old West-themed town that serves as a tourism hub
- The valley is considered one of the top Nordic ski destinations in the western US
- Mazama and Winthrop communities provide strong local support for Nordic skiing
