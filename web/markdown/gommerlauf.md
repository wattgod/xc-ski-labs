## Course

Point-to-point through the Goms Valley, following the upper Rhone River valley between traditional Valais villages. The course is predominantly flat to gently rolling, running through the wide valley floor with views of the surrounding peaks of the Bernese and Pennine Alps.

Format: point-to-point  
Surface: Machine-groomed trails along the Goms Valley floor

## Climate

High Alpine valley winter

The Goms Valley at 1,350m is one of Switzerland's coldest inhabited valleys. Temperatures regularly drop below -20°C in winter. The high altitude and cold-air pooling in the valley floor create exceptionally cold, dry conditions that preserve snow quality. February race day temperatures typically range from -15°C to -3°C.

Typical race-day temperature: -15 to -3°C

Challenges:
- High altitude at 1,350m affects performance for unacclimatized skiers
- Extreme cold from valley inversion — among the coldest spots in Switzerland
- Dry Alpine air requires extra hydration and skin protection
- Sun angle in February can create sharp shadow/sun temperature contrasts

## History

Founded in 1973, the Gommerlauf has grown into one of Switzerland's premier citizen ski races. The Goms Valley has been a Nordic skiing heartland for centuries, and the race showcases this tradition by traversing the valley between its historic villages. The event is a Euroloppet member, drawing participants from across Europe to its high-altitude course.

### Notable Facts

- The Goms Valley is where the Rhone River begins — one of Europe's great rivers
- At 1,350m, it is one of the highest marathon ski races in the Alps
- The Goms is one of Switzerland's coldest valleys — temperatures below -30°C recorded
- Traditional Valais wooden chalets line the course in villages like Oberwald and Münster
- Euroloppet member connecting Swiss skiing to the European marathon circuit
- The valley has produced multiple Swiss Olympic XC skiers
