Point-to-point mountain crossing from Rena to Lillehammer with significant climbing. The course reaches 900m+ altitude on the Birkebeiner ridge before descending into Lillehammer. Demanding elevation profile with the infamous climb to the high point.

## History

The race commemorates the 1206 rescue of the infant Prince Haakon Haakonsson by two Birkebeiner warriors who skied him across the mountains from Lillehammer to safety in Rena during the Norwegian civil war. The 3.5kg backpack requirement represents the weight of the child. Founded in 1932, it has become Norway's most celebrated ski marathon and a cornerstone of the Worldloppet series.

### Notable Facts

- Commemorates the 1206 rescue of infant Prince Haakon — the backpack weight represents the baby
- The Birkebeiner warriors' name means 'birch legs' from the birch-bark leggings they wore
- Finishes at the Lillehammer Olympic cross-country stadium from the 1994 Games
- Has inspired the American Birkebeiner (founded 1973) and the Canadian Birkebeiner
- Part of both Worldloppet and Ski Classics series
- The race has been cancelled multiple times due to extreme weather on the mountain
