## Course

Mountain course through the Jizera Mountains (Góry Izerskie), rolling terrain along the Polish-Czech border ridge. The route traverses high-altitude plateaus and forested valleys at 800-1000m elevation. Classic technique only for the main 51km distance.

## Climate

Central European mountain winter

March in the Jizera Mountains at 850m brings variable late-season conditions. The range straddles the Polish-Czech border and receives reliable snowfall, though March dates can bring warming. The mountains create their own weather patterns with frequent fog and precipitation.

Typical race-day temperature: -5 to 5°C

Challenges:
- Late-season March date increases risk of warm temperatures and soft snow

- Variable snow conditions between shaded forest and open ridges

## History

Bieg Piastów was founded in 1976 in Jakuszyce, in the Jizera Mountains of southwestern Poland. Named after the Piast dynasty — the founding royal house of Poland — the race embodies Polish national pride in cross-country skiing. It has grown to become the largest XC ski event in Central and Eastern Europe, regularly drawing around 5,000 participants. The race joined the Worldloppet series and serves as Poland's flagship entry on the circuit. Jakuszyce itself has developed into Poland's primary cross-country skiing center.

### Notable Facts

- Named after the Piast dynasty, which ruled Poland from the 10th to 14th centuries
- Poland's largest cross-country ski race and a source of national sporting pride
- Jakuszyce holds the Polish record for lowest temperature: -36.9°C in 1929
- The Jizera Mountains straddle the Polish-Czech-German tri-border region
- Justyna Kowalczyk, Poland's Olympic XC skiing champion, trained in this region
- The race has been a Worldloppet member since the series expanded to Eastern Europe
