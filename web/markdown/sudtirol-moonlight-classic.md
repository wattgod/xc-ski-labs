## Course

A looped course across Europe's largest high-altitude plateau, the Seiser Alm (Alpe di Siusi), illuminated by 1,000 torches under a full moon. Mass start at 8 PM.

Format: loop  
Surface: Machine-groomed classic tracks on high-altitude Alpine plateau  
Technical rating: 2/5  
Grooming: Excellent Dolomite resort-standard grooming

## Climate

Alpine Dolomite winter

The Seiser Alm plateau sits at 1,800-2,000m elevation, providing reliable winter conditions. Night temperatures on the plateau during the full moon race typically drop to -10°C to -5°C. Clear skies are needed for the full atmospheric effect.

Typical race-day temperature: -10 to -3°C

Challenges:
- Night racing in subzero Alpine temperatures
- Altitude at 1,800-2,000m can affect unacclimatized skiers
- Wax selection critical for cold night conditions
- Wind exposure on the open plateau

## History

The Südtirol Moonlight Classic was first held in 2009 on the Seiser Alm, Europe's largest high-altitude Alpine plateau. The concept of a torchlit night race timed to the full moon quickly captured the imagination of the Nordic ski community. The event has grown into one of the most atmospheric ski races in the world, combining South Tyrolean tradition with Dolomite spectacle. It joined the Ski Classics Challengers circuit, bringing international visibility.

### Notable Facts

- First edition in 2009 on Europe's largest high-altitude plateau
- Course illuminated by 1,000 torches under the full moon
- Mass start at 8 PM — one of very few major night XC ski races
