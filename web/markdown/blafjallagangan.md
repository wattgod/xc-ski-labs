## Course

A 40km classic race across the Bláfjöll highlands near Reykjavik. The course traverses Iceland's unique volcanic landscape — moss-covered lava fields blanketed in snow, with views to distant glaciers and the North Atlantic. The terrain is rolling with moderate elevation change, but the defining feature is the wind exposure on the treeless highland terrain. Unlike any other ski race in the world.

Format: loop  
Surface: Groomed classic tracks over volcanic highland terrain  
Technical rating: 3/5  
Grooming: Good standard given challenging conditions; wind can affect track quality

## Climate

Sub-Arctic maritime

Spring in Iceland's highlands is unpredictable. The Bláfjöll area sits at moderate altitude near Reykjavik, exposed to Atlantic weather systems. Typically -5°C to 3°C. Wind is the dominant factor — Iceland's open terrain offers no shelter. Snow conditions vary from powder to ice depending on wind and temperature cycles.

Typical race-day temperature: -5 to 3°C

Challenges:
- Extreme wind exposure on open volcanic terrain
- Rapidly changing weather — sun to blizzard in minutes
- Snow quality varies dramatically with wind and temperature
- Maritime influence can bring rain even in spring
- Volcanic terrain creates unique course challenges

## History

A relatively new event, Bláfjallagangan launched around 2018 and became a Euroloppet member in 2020. The race takes place in the Bláfjöll highlands, just 35 kilometers from Reykjavik, making it the most accessible ski race in Iceland. The volcanic landscape creates a race experience unlike anything else in the skiing world. Iceland's growing interest in cross-country skiing, combined with Euroloppet recognition, has helped establish this race as a unique destination event.
