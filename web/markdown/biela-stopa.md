## Course

A highland course through the Kremnica Mountains between 860m and 1,230m altitude. The terrain is rolling with moderate climbs through mixed spruce and beech forests. The course traverses the Skalka plateau with views across the central Slovak highlands. A genuine mountain marathon that tests fitness and resilience.

Format: loop  
Surface: Machine-groomed tracks through mountain forest and highland plateau

## Climate

Central European mountain winter

February in the Kremnica Mountains at 860-1,230m altitude brings cold continental conditions. Temperatures range from -12°C to -2°C. Snow is generally reliable at altitude but the mid-mountain location can experience freeze-thaw cycles. Wind exposure on ridge sections adds chill factor.

Typical race-day temperature: -12 to -2°C

Challenges:

## History

Founded around 1973, Biela Stopa ('White Track') is Slovakia's oldest cross-country ski event. Set in the Kremnica Mountains of central Slovakia, the race has endured through the Czechoslovak era, the Velvet Divorce, and EU accession, maintaining its identity as an authentic Central European mountain marathon. Joining the Euroloppet series in 1994 brought international recognition to this storied event.

### Notable Facts

- Slovakia's oldest cross-country ski event — founded circa 1973
- Name means 'White Track' in Slovak
- Euroloppet member since 1994
- Survived the transition from Czechoslovakia to independent Slovakia
- Kremnica is historically famous for its medieval gold mint
- Course traverses the Skalka plateau above the historic mining town
