## Course

A 42km classic race through the forests and valleys surrounding Ushuaia, the world's southernmost city. The course traverses Tierra del Fuego's distinctive sub-Antarctic landscape — lenga beech forests, frozen valleys, and mountain views with the Beagle Channel in the distance. The terrain is rolling with moderate climbs through forested sections. Wind exposure on open stretches is the defining course challenge.

Format: loop  
Surface: Groomed classic tracks through sub-Antarctic terrain; conditions variable  
Technical rating: 3/5  
Grooming: Basic to moderate — limited grooming equipment in remote Patagonian location

## Climate

Sub-Antarctic maritime winter

August is mid-winter in the Southern Hemisphere. Ushuaia's sub-Antarctic climate delivers cold, wet, and windy conditions. Typically -5°C to 2°C. Snow reliability can be inconsistent — Tierra del Fuego's maritime influence brings rain-snow transitions. Fierce Patagonian winds are the defining challenge.

Typical race-day temperature: -5 to 2°C

Challenges:

## History

Founded in 1986, the Ushuaia Loppet holds the distinction of being the world's most southern ski race, set in Tierra del Fuego at the southern tip of Argentina. The race was a Worldloppet member, though membership is currently suspended. Racing in August during the Southern Hemisphere winter, it offers a unique counter-seasonal option for Northern Hemisphere skiers. The race traverses the sub-Antarctic landscapes around Ushuaia, a city that brands itself as 'El Fin del Mundo' — the End of the World.
