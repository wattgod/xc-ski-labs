## Course

Point-to-point from the former copper mining town of Sulitjelma to Fauske on the Norwegian coast. The classic-only course traverses Arctic mountain terrain with significant climbing through birch forest and above the treeline. The descent to Fauske delivers a dramatic finish.

## History

Founded in 1968, Kobberløpet is named after the copper mining heritage of Sulitjelma, a former mining town nestled in the mountains of Nordland above the Arctic Circle. The race traverses the route between Sulitjelma and the coastal town of Fauske, a journey that miners and their families would have made on skis for generations. Now part of the Ski Classics Challengers series, Kobberløpet attracts both elite long-distance racers and citizen skiers seeking an authentic Arctic mountain experience.

### Notable Facts

- Named after copper (kobber) — Sulitjelma was a major copper mining center from 1891 to 1991
- Part of the Ski Classics Challengers series
- Above the Arctic Circle — one of the northernmost races in the Ski Classics network
- Sulitjelma mines operated for exactly 100 years before closing in 1991
- April timing offers up to 16 hours of daylight above the Arctic Circle
- The point-to-point route from mountains to coast mirrors historic travel routes
- Founded in 1968 — approaching 60 years of continuous racing
