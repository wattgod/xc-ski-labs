## Course

Loops through the Tamarack Cross Country Ski Center trails and surrounding Sierra terrain. The course winds through lodgepole pine forest and open meadows with views of the Sherwin Range, Mammoth Mountain, and the eastern escarpment of the Sierra Nevada.

Format: loop  
Surface: Machine-groomed trails through Sierra forest and meadow terrain  
Technical rating: 3/5  
Grooming: Good grooming by Tamarack Cross Country Ski Center, well-maintained classic and skate tracks

## Climate

High Sierra alpine winter

At 2,440 meters, Mammoth sits in one of the snowiest locations in California. The Eastern Sierra receives both Pacific storms and cold continental air. Race day temperatures range from -15°C to 0°C. Intense high-altitude sun and thin air are defining features.

Typical race-day temperature: -12 to -2°C

Challenges:
- Significant altitude at 2,440m — acclimatization matters
- Intense UV exposure at high elevation
- Thin air reduces performance 10-15% for lowland skiers
- Pacific storms can bring heavy snowfall during race week
- Temperature inversions can create extreme cold in valley bottom

## History

Founded in 1978, the Mammoth Marathon is one of the oldest Nordic ski marathons in the American West. It grew out of the Mammoth Lakes cross-country skiing community, which has thrived due to the area's legendary snowfall — the Eastern Sierra routinely receives over 10 meters of snow annually. The race has persisted for nearly five decades despite the challenges of organizing a high-altitude winter event, becoming a fixture of the California Nordic scene.

### Notable Facts

- Founded in 1978 — one of the oldest Nordic marathons in the western US
- At 2,440m, it is the highest-altitude Nordic marathon in America
- Mammoth Lakes receives an average of 10+ meters of snowfall annually
- The Eastern Sierra has been a training ground for US Ski Team members
- The Tamarack Cross Country Ski Center has operated since the 1970s
