## Climate

Heavy snowfall maritime (Sea of Japan effect)

Sapporo receives some of the heaviest snowfall of any major city on Earth thanks to cold Siberian air crossing the Sea of Japan. February temperatures range from -10°C to -2°C. Snow depth regularly exceeds 1 meter in the city, with deeper accumulation in surrounding hills. The snow is drier and lighter than typical maritime snow.

Typical race-day temperature: -8 to -2°C

Challenges:
- Heavy fresh snowfall can soften tracks mid-race
- Deep powder alongside groomed trails if you fall
- Cold Siberian air mass brings sudden temperature drops
- Humidity from Sea of Japan effect affects waxing

## History

Founded in 1981, the Sapporo Ski Marathon capitalizes on the cross-country skiing infrastructure built for the 1972 Winter Olympics. Sapporo, one of the snowiest cities on Earth, provides unmatched natural snow conditions. The race has become Japan's premier cross-country ski marathon and a Worldloppet member, attracting both Japanese recreational skiers and international Worldloppet passport collectors drawn by the unique combination of Japanese culture and Hokkaido powder.

### Notable Facts

- Built on infrastructure from the 1972 Sapporo Winter Olympics
- Sapporo receives over 5 meters of snowfall annually — among the snowiest cities on Earth
- Worldloppet member event, the only one in Japan
- Coincides with the famous Sapporo Snow Festival in early February
- Showcases Japan's unique cross-country skiing culture on Hokkaido
